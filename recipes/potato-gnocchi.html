Potato Gnocchi

The Glorious Pasta of Italy

Ingredients

  • 2 pound(s) potato unpeeled (about 4 large potatoes)
  • 1.75 cup(s) all-purpose flour
  • 1 unit(s) egg(s) large, slightly beaten
  • 0.75 teaspoon(s) salt
  • 1 tablespoon(s) grappa optional
  • 1 unit(s) semolina for dusting baking sheets

Directions

  1. Put the tomatoes in a large pot and add cold water to cover by 1 inch. Place the pot over high heat and bring to a boil. Reduce the heat to medium-high and boil for 30 minutes or until potatoes are tender.
  2. Drain the potatoes in a colander in the sink. As soon as they are cool enough to handle, peel, them, cut them in half, and force them through a potato ricer onto a clean work surface, forming an airy mound.
  3. Sprinkle slightly less than the 1-3/4 C flour all around the base of the mound (use a little bit less if you are not using grappa). Make a small well in the center of the mound and carefully pour the egg into it. Sprinkle the salt over the egg and pour in the grappa.
  4. With a fork or your fingers, begin to incorporate the egg and grappa gently into the potatoes, eventually incorporating the flour as well.
  5. Very lightly knead the mixture until it forms a soft ball of dough. It should feel pliant, slightly tacky, and somewhat shaggy, rather than completely smooth. Cover the dough with a clean tea towel and use a dough scraper to remove any stuck bits of dough and flour from the work surface.
  6. Sprinkle a small amount of plain flour onto the work surface. Slice off a piece of dough about the size of a tangerine. Using your palms, gently roll the piece of dough into a rope about 3/4 inch in diameter. It rolls out easily, so don't use too much pressure. You don't want to compress the dough. Use the dough scraper to cut the rope into 1 inch pieces, no larger.
  7. Dust at least two rimmed baking sheets with semolina. This is where you will put the gnocchi once you have shaped them.
  8. With one hand, grasp a stainless steel table fork, holding it vertical with the handle up, the concave part facing you, and the tines against the flour-dusted work surface. With your other hand, grasp a piece of dough between your thumb and middle finger. Roll the piece of dough down the tines of the fork, using your middle finger to propel it downward and, at the same time, create a small groove or indentation in it. When you are done, you should have a the groove from your finger on once side and the ridge from the fork tines on the other.
  9. Transfer the dumpling to a baking sheet. Continue to shape the remaining cut pieces of dough and transfer them, in a single layer and not touching, to the tray. Shape the rest of the dough in the same way.
  10. Warm the sauce. Bring a very large pot of water to a rolling boil and salt generously. The gnocchi cook very quickly, so have read a warmed shallow serving bowl. Spread a ladleful or two of sauce in the bottom of the bowl.
  11. Gently drop the gnocchi into the boiling water. In a couple of minutes, even before the water returns to a boil, some of the gnocchi will begin to float to the surface. Taste one. It should be soft and fluffy but cooked throughout.
  12. A couple of seconds after you see some gnocchi float to the surface, use a large slotted spoon to transfer them to the warmed serving bowl. It will take 1-2 minutes for all gnocchi to float to the surface.
  13. Once all of the gnocchi are in the bowl, spoon the remaining sauce over them. Sprinkler a little pecorino on top and serve immediately.
  14. The gnocchi may be made in advance and frozen uncooked. Place them, in a single layer and not touching, on semolina-dusted rimmed baking sheets, put them in the freezer, and freeze for 1 hour. Transfer them to large zip-lock freezer bags or tightly lidded containers and freeze for up to 1 month, then cook directly from the freezer.
