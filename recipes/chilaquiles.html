Chilaquiles

Alton Brown

Ingredients

  • 21 tablespoon(s) vegetable oil
  • 6 unit(s) corn tortilla(s) each torn into 4 to 6 pieces
  • 12 ounce(s) fresh salsa or roasted chile salsa
  • 0.5 teaspoon(s) salt
  • 4 unit(s) egg(s) large
  • 0.25 cup(s) cotija cheese
  • 1 tablespoon(s) cilantro chopped

Directions

  1. Place a rack about 6 inches under the broiler and heat the boiler to high.
  2. Heat the vegetable oil over medium heat in a 10- to 12-inch cast-iron skillet until the oil barely shimmers. Add the tortilla pieces, a few at a time, to the pan. Once they’re all in, they’ll overlap, but move them around and flip them with tongs every now and then and they’ll fry evenly.
  3. When the chips are crisp and slightly brown around the edges, ladle in the salsa and 1/4 teaspoon of the salt. If you prefer your chilaquiles on the crunchy side, go with 9 ounces of the salsa; if you like them soft, go with 12 ounces.
  4. Reduce the heat and allow the salsa to simmer and absorb into the chips a bit, 5 minutes. Since they’re fried, the chips will hold together, forming layers.
  5. Arrange the softened tortillas so that you can crack the eggs evenly across the surface, one in each quarter of the pan. Sprinkle with the remaining 1/4 teaspoon salt.
  6. Park the pan under the broiler for 3 minutes or until the whites set. The yolks will still be a bit runny.
  7. Remove the skillet and scatter the surface of the chilaquiles with the cheese and cilantro. Deliver to the table and serve right out of the skillet.
